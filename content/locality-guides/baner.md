## Location and borders

Baner is a suburb in the western part of Pune. Wikipedia places it in the city's Western Metropolitan Corridor, bordered by Pashan to the south, Balewadi to the west, Aundh to the north and Pune University to the east [1]. The Ramnadi, an 18-km river that also passes Pashan and Aundh, flows through Baner [1][2].

## History and land

Wikipedia traces Baner back to the 17th century, when the Kalamkar family held the area, and credits Kavaji Kalamkar with a temple of the goddess Tukai Mata on the hillock now called Baner Hill [1]. The area was long known for farming, with mango, guava, tamarind and Indian plums among its fruit. After about 2005 the arrival of IT firms and residential complexes began to erode that reputation [1]. Baner Hill and its spur, Pashan Hill, are the site of the Baner–Pashan Biodiversity Park, which Wikipedia describes as a proposed 200-hectare protected area under development by the Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC). The same page says volunteers and locals work on a plantation drive there on Sunday mornings [3].

## Civic administration

Baner lies within Pune Municipal Corporation limits. In 2019 the mayor said that the stretches of the Ramnadi through Baner, Aundh, Balewadi and Bavdhan were "under civic limits" and that PMC would support the river clean-up [2]. Wikipedia likewise names PMC as the civic agency for Baner [1].

## Employers and housing

Wikipedia calls Baner a residential and commercial hub where large portions are occupied by IT companies [1]. In August 2026 Punekar News described Baner–Balewadi as one of Pune's major residential and IT hubs. It also reported residents saying that housing and commercial development in recent years had outpaced roads and traffic management [4].

## Roads and traffic

Residents quoted in August 2026 named Dasara Chowk, Mamta Chowk, Radha Chowk, Kalamkar Chowk and Nannavare Chowk, along with a stretch of Baner Road, as congestion points. They also pointed to school pick-up and drop-off times and to roadside vegetable and fish markets as contributing factors [4]. The Baner-Balewadi Nagrik Manch, a citizens' group, has asked PMC for a traffic impact study before the proposed Balewadi–Kaspate Wasti bridge opens. The bridge is expected to ease travel between Pimpri-Chinchwad and Baner-Balewadi, and the group warns it could bring many more vehicles into Balewadi [5].

## Metro status

Pune Metro Line 3 will serve Baner, but its Baner stations are not in the first group to open. On 15 September 2026 Punekar News reported that Laxmi Nagar, Balewadi Phata, Baner Gaon, Baner, Reserve Bank of India and Shivajinagar would stay closed at first because work was pending [6]. The safety regulator cleared the first 12 stations, from Maan to R.K. Laxman Museum at Balewadi, on 23 July 2026 [7]. By 29 September 2026 no start date had been confirmed, and the remaining stations were expected by December 2026 [8].

## Everyday life

Healthcare in Baner includes Manipal Hospital, which introduced a robotic surgery system in April 2025 [10], and the Apulki Medical Centre, a 150-bed cancer and cardiac hospital developed by PMC with Apulki Healthcare under a public-private partnership and inaugurated on 17 May 2026 [9]. Wikipedia names the Bhairavnath temple as the town deity's temple, with a festival each Akshaya Tritiya, and describes a wide range of restaurants, cafés and pubs [1]. Residents also mention roadside vegetable and fish markets [4].
