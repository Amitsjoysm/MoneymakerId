## Where Hadapsar is

Hadapsar is a suburb in eastern Pune, with the postal codes 411028 and 411013 [1]. The Pune–Solapur national highway, listed on Wikipedia as NH 65, runs through it, and a bypass through Kharadi connects the area to the Pune–Nagar highway [1]. Two planned townships, Magarpatta and Amanora Park Town, are described as lying within Hadapsar [2][3]. To the south of the suburb is a hill called Tukai Tekdi, named after the goddess Tukai, whose temple stands on it [1].

## How the area developed

Hadapsar has a long recorded history. Wikipedia notes that a battle of the Second Anglo-Maratha War was fought here on 25 October 1802, in which Yashwantrao Holkar defeated the combined armies of Peshwa Baji Rao II and Daulat Rao Scindia [1]. The same article says the area developed as an industrial zone from about 1990, and it names the Hadapsar Industrial Estate and companies such as Serum Institute of India, Bharat Forge and Honeywell among the organisations based there [1].

More recent growth has come through large township projects. Magarpatta is a 182-hectare gated community where construction began in 2000 and, according to Wikipedia, was still continuing in 2025 [2]. Amanora Park Town is a residential township spread over about 400 acres and developed by The City Corporation Limited [3].

## Housing, offices and everyday life

Wikipedia's Magarpatta article lists a commercial area, residential clusters, a hospital with several specialisations, schools, a ten-hectare park called Aditi Gardens and a shopping mall, with around 30 per cent of the area under green cover [2]. The Hadapsar article also names Amanora Town Centre as a mall within Amanora Park Town [1].

For daily travel, Hadapsar Gadital is described as a central PMPML bus station, with routes that include air-conditioned services towards Katraj, Hinjewadi, Nigdi and Wagholi, while state transport buses stop on the Solapur and Saswad highways [1]. Hadapsar railway station lies on the Mumbai Dadar–Solapur section; local trains heading south towards Daund and Solapur stop there, and some long-distance trains begin or end their journeys at the station [1][4].

## Metro and road projects, with dates

None of the sources reviewed for this guide reports a metro station open in Hadapsar. The status of the planned projects, as reported, is:
- **26 November 2025:** the Union Cabinet approved Line 4 (Kharadi–Hadapsar–Swargate–Khadakwasla) and spur Line 4A (Nal Stop–Warje–Manik Baug), together 31.636 km with 28 elevated stations, to be completed within five years. The announcement says pre-construction surveys and design consultancy were already under way and that the line will have an interchange at Hadapsar Railway Station [5].
- **4 November 2025:** the Maharashtra Cabinet Infrastructure Committee approved two further corridors, from Hadapsar to Loni Kalbhor and from Hadapsar Bus Depot to Saswad Road [6].
- **Reported 11 February 2026:** MahaMetro had invited bids for design consultants on those corridors: an 11.35 km Hadapsar–Loni Kalbhor stretch with 10 stations and a 5.57 km Hadapsar–Saswad Road stretch with four stations, with bids due to open on 5 March 2026 [7].
- **Reported 17 July 2026:** the existing Magarpatta flyover is to be demolished and replaced by a double-decker flyover for the Swargate–Hadapsar metro, with work expected to start in January 2027 after traffic planning with PMC and the traffic police. The same report says the state approved a 31.5 km six-lane elevated road from Hadapsar to Yavat along the Pune–Solapur highway, with construction expected to begin the following year and take four to five years [8].

## Civic administration

Hadapsar is part of the area administered by the Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC). Wikipedia records that in July 2017 eleven surrounding villages, among them Hadapsar (Sadesatra Nali) and Phursungi, were added to PMC limits [9]. In September 2024 the Free Press Journal reported that the state government had asked PMC for its opinion on forming a new municipal corporation for the Hadapsar and Wagholi areas, and that the villages of Uruli Devachi and Fursungi had recently been removed from PMC limits to form a separate municipal council [10]. This guide records no later decision, so readers should check the current position with PMC.
