## Location and setting

Hinjewadi, also spelt Hinjawadi, is a suburb on the north-western side of the Pune region. It is known for the Rajiv Gandhi Infotech Park, a business park of about 2,800 acres that the Maharashtra Industrial Development Corporation (MIDC) built [1]. Wakad is linked to it by a bridge called the Wakad–Hinjawadi Bridge, and the Mumbai–Bengaluru highway bypass runs near both areas [9]. Nearby villages such as Maan and Marunji are named alongside Hinjewadi in civic discussions [4][5].

## How the area developed

A 2017 commentary by ANAROCK Property Consultants describes Hinjewadi as a group of small villages on the edge of Pune before the park arrived. It points to the state's early-2000s IT policy and a stamp-duty waiver on properties built on MIDC land as helping the large-scale development that followed [2]. The same piece put the office mix at that time at roughly 85 per cent IT and IT-enabled services, 10 per cent manufacturing and 5 per cent biotechnology [2]. The park is organised in numbered phases. Wikipedia lists Cognizant and Tata Technologies among occupiers in Phase 1, Infosys and Wipro in Phase 2, and Tata Consultancy Services, Tech Mahindra and Capgemini in Phase 3 [1].

## Housing and daily travel

According to the 2017 commentary, housing grew around the park largely as self-contained townships, and about 14,000 residential units were launched between 2013 and the third quarter of 2017 [2]. Daily travel into and out of the area is heavy. In September 2026 the Pune district collector put the number of people travelling to and from Hinjewadi each day at around five to six lakh [3].

## Roads and traffic

The district administration links congestion to the small number of entry and exit routes into the park [3]. In September 2025 Pimpri-Chinchwad traffic police introduced an experiment in which the Wakad–Hinjawadi bridge works as a one-way bridge during peak office hours, with the direction reversed between morning and evening; the report called it temporary and open to feedback, so its present status should be checked [9]. Plans reported in September 2026 include six new parallel roads near the Bhujbal, Laxmi and Hinjewadi junctions, where land acquisition had begun, and about 60 km of drainage network with footpaths and streetlights, proposed over roughly eighteen months [3]. Heavy vehicles carrying construction material are already restricted during the morning and evening peaks [4].

## Metro status

Pune Metro Line 3 is a 23-km corridor from Hinjewadi to Shivajinagar through Wakad, Balewadi and Baner [6]. It had not opened to passengers in the latest reports checked. The Commissioner of Metro Rail Safety cleared the first 12 stations, from Maan to R.K. Laxman Museum at Balewadi, on 23 July 2026 [7]. On 29 September 2026 the Free Press Journal reported that 17 of the 23 stations were expected to be ready, that no start date had been confirmed, and that the remaining stations were expected by December 2026 [8].

## Who runs the area

Hinjewadi does not sit under a single municipal corporation. In June 2025 Punekar News described a patchwork of MIDC, PMRDA, the gram panchayat and the Public Works Department. It also reported, citing unnamed sources, that the state had cleared a long-pending proposal from 2015 to bring Hinjewadi and nearby villages into Pimpri-Chinchwad (PCMC) limits [5]. A high-power committee meeting reported on 4 September 2026 decided that waste from the area will be disposed of through PCMC [4]. ## Everyday life

The same committee discussed the need for playgrounds, a cultural centre and green public spaces [4], and the water supply options under discussion include the Mulshi and Pawna dams [3][4]. A large retail mall, Phoenix Mall of the Millennium, was unveiled in neighbouring Wakad, and its developer tied the area's infrastructure growth to the Hinjewadi park [10]. PMPML buses connect the park to parts of the city, including Baner, Kothrud, Hadapsar and Kharadi [1].
