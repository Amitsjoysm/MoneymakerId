## Where Wagholi is

Wagholi is a suburb on the eastern edge of Pune, on the Pune–Nagar highway [3]. It should not be confused with a village of the same name in Koregaon taluka of Satara district [2]. The postal index number for Wagholi near Pune is 412207, and Wikipedia traces the name to the Marathi word "wagh", meaning tiger, and to the Wagheshwar temple [1]. A 2018 column by a property consultant put Pune Airport about 11 km away by the Lohegaon–Wagholi road and described Kharadi, the nearby IT hub, as one of the reasons for the area's growth [3].

## How the area developed

In 2018 Wagholi still fell under a gram panchayat, the village-level body [3]. According to the head of research at a property consultancy writing in January 2018, the housing boom began because land parcels were available, registration costs were low and legal procedures were simple in a gram panchayat area. He dated the area's transformation to 2007 and linked it to the growth of IT and IT-enabled services in eastern Pune [3]. He reported about 22,000 residential units launched between 2013 and the third quarter of 2017, more than 80 per cent of them in 2013 to 2015 [3]. That column reflects an industry viewpoint and pre-dates the area's merger with the city corporation.

## Civic administration

Wagholi is now inside Pune Municipal Corporation (PMC) limits. A draft notification issued on 23 December 2020 listed Wagholi among 23 villages proposed for merger [4], and the Observer Research Foundation records that 23 villages were notified for merger on 29 June 2021 [5]. A residents' association, the Wagholi Housing Societies Association, gives 1 July 2021 as the date of the merger and describes the population as more than three lakh people [6].

The arrangement has not been settled to everyone's satisfaction. In December 2022 the association opposed a reported attempt to separate Wagholi from PMC [6]. In September 2024 the Free Press Journal reported that the state government had asked PMC for its opinion on a new municipal corporation for Hadapsar and Wagholi, or on a separate municipal council for Wagholi [12]. In August 2025 PMC's draft ward plan divided Wagholi between two wards, one combined with Kharadi and the other with Lohegaon–Viman Nagar; the association said the area has over 250 housing societies and that the plan relied on 2011 Census figures [7]. This guide records no final outcome on the separation question.

## Roads and getting around

Nagar Road, described in 2018 as six lanes and 15 km long from Bund Garden to Wagholi, gives access to Viman Nagar, Kalyani Nagar and Kharadi [3]. In April 2026 PMC's standing committee approved a proposal to develop the Kesnand Phata–Dube Nagar Phata road in Wagholi, known as Road No. 68, as a "world-class" model road with wider carriageway, footpaths, drainage and street lighting; timelines and budget were still to be finalised [8].

## Metro, with dates

No metro line was reported as running to Wagholi in the sources reviewed. The projects, as reported, are:
- **25 June 2025:** the Union Cabinet approved Corridor 2B, Ramwadi to Wagholi/Vitthalwadi, together with Corridor 2A, Vanaz to Chandani Chowk. The two corridors span 12.75 km with 13 stations and are scheduled for completion within four years. The announcement adds that intercity buses from Ahilya Nagar and Chhatrapati Sambhaji Nagar are planned to connect at Wagholi [9].
- **6 February 2026:** the Free Press Journal described a 4.7 km double-decker structure on Nagar Road between Ramwadi and Tuljabhavani Nagar, with a six-lane elevated road at the first level and the metro above it [10].
- **Reported 17 July 2026:** Maha Metro had invited bids for four elevated stations (Kalyaninagar, Somnath Nagar, Kharadi and Tuljabhavani Chowk) on the 11.6 km corridor, which will eventually have 11 stations. The first phase runs only from Ramwadi to Tuljabhavani Chowk, and the project was at the contractor-selection stage [11].

## Everyday life

Press reports from 2024 and 2025 describe road connectivity, water supply, drainage and garbage disposal as continuing concerns for residents [7][12]. In 2024 a director of the housing association said the area has schools, colleges and other establishments, and that residents were having a hard time because of a lack of facilities [12]. Readers comparing localities should check current conditions on the ground, since civic projects here are still moving from approval to delivery.
